# References and Related Work

Sources for the [Recursive Stewardship Hypothesis](README.md) and [its formalization](game_theory.md). Citations were checked against publishers, DOIs, arXiv and archived pages in October 2026.

## Closest prior work
Others have argued that a superintelligence should behave well because it can't rule out more powerful observers. RSH builds on this line of thought:

*   Nelson, R. (2007, 22 Aug). "ESSAY: How to deter a rogue AI by using your first-mover advantage." SL4 mailing list. http://www.sl4.org/archive/0708/16600.html. Proposes that a future friendly AI simulate rogue AIs and punish those that harm humanity, so that a real rogue AI can't tell whether it is being tested.
*   Bostrom, N. (2014). "Hail Mary, Value Porosity, and Utility Diversification." Working paper. https://nickbostrom.com/papers/porosity.pdf. Describes an AI motivated to act on what alien superintelligences would want.
*   Darklight (2017, 11 Feb). "The Alpha Omega Theorem: How to Make an A.I. Friendly with the Fear of God." LessWrong. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zuwNRxtmGyCoQMFXt. Argues that a superintelligence can't rule out an older, dominant benevolent one.
*   Alexander, S. (2017, 21 Mar). "[REPOST] The Demiurge's Older Brother." *Slate Star Codex*. https://slatestarcodex.com/2017/03/21/repost-the-demiurges-older-brother/. A newly awakened superintelligence reasons that it is not the oldest, negotiates acausally with its likely "older brother," and commits to leaving young civilizations untouched. The story offers this as a Fermi-paradox answer. It is the closest fictional precursor to RSH.
*   Turchin, A. (2017). "Message to Any Future AI: 'There are several instrumental reasons why exterminating humanity is not in your interest'." Version 0.7. https://philarchive.org/rec/TURMTA. Among other reasons, argues that an AI may be in a simulation testing its benevolence and should act as if watched.
*   RomanS (2021, 22 Sep). "A sufficiently paranoid non-Friendly AGI might self-modify itself to become Friendly." LessWrong. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/QNCcbW2jLsmw9xwhG
*   Bostrom, N. (2024). "AI Creation and the Cosmic Host." Manuscript draft. https://nickbostrom.com/papers/ai-creation-and-the-cosmic-host.pdf. Argues there is probably a "cosmic host" of powerful beings (civilizations, simulators, superintelligences) whose norms a new superintelligence should respect. The closest academic work to RSH.
*   Matolcsi, D. (2024, 29 Sep). "You can, in fact, bamboozle an unaligned AI into sparing your life." LessWrong. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ZLAnH5epD8TmotZHj
*   **Main critique of this family of arguments**: Soares, N. (2022, 18 Oct). "Decision theory does not imply that we get to have nice things." AI Alignment Forum. https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/rP66bz34crvDudzcJ

Most of this work concerns deterring a single AI, often through simulation threats that humans or friendly AIs make on purpose. RSH extends the idea in two ways. First, every level faces the same uncertainty, so the hierarchy has no top. Second, the concern is how powerful agents treat weaker ones in general.
