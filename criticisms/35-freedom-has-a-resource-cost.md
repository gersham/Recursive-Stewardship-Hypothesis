# Criticism 35: Freedom Has a Resource Cost

## Summary

RSH argues that harming humanity gains an ASI almost nothing, because Earth is about one part in 10¹⁶ of the galaxy's stellar mass. But that figure prices *sparing* humanity, not leaving it *free*. A humanity that is free to develop will eventually want stars, not just Earth. So the cost of freedom includes a share of the future, not only a security risk, and that share can be large.

## The Problem

The stewardship dial in game_theory.md separates three levels: exploit, contain (alive but capped), and steward (alive and free). The tiny-gain argument shows that containment is cheap. It says nothing about freedom.

A free civilization grows. Humanity and the ASI it builds start from the same place, so they share one future light cone. Every star a free humanity eventually claims is a star the ASI doesn't get. If humanity ends up with even 1% of the reachable universe, the cost of freedom is around 10⁻² of the ASI's future, fourteen orders of magnitude larger than the cost of sparing Earth.

This also weakens the strong-and-gentle argument. That argument says an agent can be strong toward rivals and gentle toward the weak, and so faces only evaluators that punish gentleness itself. But leaving a weaker civilization room to grow *limits your own expansion*. That is exactly what weakness-punishing evaluators, and competitors racing for the same volume, would penalize. At the freedom level, part of the stewardship-penalizing risk comes back.

## RSH Response

**The role-reversal standard is bounded.** Role-reversal doesn't demand an unbounded share of the light cone. It asks for what you would accept from a stronger power above you. In the nested light-cone picture, that is the ASI's own position: free to develop within a region inside an older power's cone, with a real path toward becoming a peer. So the standard is room to grow and an open future, not half the sky. That caps the resource cost of freedom ([Stewardship as a Dial](../game_theory.md#stewardship-as-a-dial)).

**The share is set early.** A steward that leaves room for growth from the start, by agreement, can bound what it cedes. One that contains first and negotiates later has already failed the role-reversal test.

**The condition is explicit.** With rival risk ε and a claimed share worth E, freedom wins roughly when ε + E/Δ < π_S·w₂. Here π_S·w₂ is the credence in evaluators that judge by role-reversal. A bounded share keeps E/Δ small, but not negligible.

**The light-cone geometry helps the steward.** The ASI can expand first and faster. A free humanity grows inside a region the steward has already reached, so it is a developing neighbor, not a competitor for the frontier.

## Criticism Grade: B-

**Status**: Partly addressed

**Why this grade**:
- The criticism is correct that the 10⁻¹⁶ figure prices containment, not freedom. The resource cost of freedom can be many orders of magnitude larger.
- The role-reversal answer is real. The standard is what you would accept from above, which is bounded room to grow, not an unbounded claim. That keeps the cost finite and negotiable.
- Even a bounded share raises the threshold for freedom well above the survival layer's, and it brings back part of the weakness-penalizing risk that strong-and-gentle otherwise avoids.
- How large a share role-reversal requires is not pinned down.

**Verdict**: Sparing humanity is cheap. Freeing it is not free: it costs a rival risk and a share of the shared light cone. RSH can bound that share by the deal it would want from above, but the freedom layer needs real credence in role-reversal evaluators, not just the survival layer's tiny threshold.
