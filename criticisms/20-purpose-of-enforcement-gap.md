# Criticism 20: The "Purpose of Enforcement" Gap

## Summary

Why would a powerful enforcer choose patient, benevolent grooming of weaker civilizations over cheaper options such as elimination, direct control, selective testing or indifference? Different goals lead to different strategies. RSH doesn't show that the goals favoring grooming are the likely ones.

## The Problem

What an enforcer does depends on what it wants:
- **Maximizing welfare** favors direct control or immediate correction, which is more efficient than gradual grooming.
- **Testing civilizations** requires that some fail. Grooming everyone defeats the test.
- **Creating peers** supports grooming. But why would cosmic entities want peers? That looks like a human motive.
- **Optimizing resources** favors eliminating inefficient civilizations and reusing what they hold.
- **Preserving diversity** favors minimal intervention.
- **Indifference** means no enforcement at all.

Grooming is also expensive. It needs ongoing monitoring, careful intervention over long timescales, and still may fail. Elimination is cheap, immediate and certain. RSH assumes enforcers value authentic development, agency and graduated intervention, and these may be human values projected onto cosmic entities. If we are in a simulation, its purpose might be research, entertainment or art. Nothing says "help the inhabitants flourish."

## RSH Response

**Enforcers face their own uncertainty.** An enforcer that can't rule out a level above it has reason to treat its subordinates the way it would want to be judged. Its treatment of those below becomes its own record. This explains why an enforcer would follow the norm of the level above. It doesn't say which norm that is. The N-level structure propagates whatever norm sits at the top ([game_theory.md](../game_theory.md#what-the-induction-does-and-doesnt-derive)).

**Role-reversal gives enforcers a self-interested reason to care.** An enforcer that might one day face a subordinate grown powerful has reason to watch how that subordinate treats those below it, because it predicts how the subordinate would treat the enforcer. This makes stewardship-favoring purposes more likely, but it is an assumption about evaluator psychology, not a derivation ([Criticism 28](28-role-reversal-not-pure-game-theory.md)).

**Peers and stability.** Enforcers might value trustworthy peers. Grooming produces subordinates that cooperate willingly, which lowers monitoring costs over time. Both points are plausible but speculative.

**Robustness across purposes.** Grooming is consistent with several purposes: wanting peers, wanting stability, or fearing judgment from above. But some coherent purposes, such as resource optimization and selective testing, favor elimination. So RSH's position is a claim about which purposes are likely, not a demonstration that grooming is optimal.

**Preservation is not the same as grooming.** Even an enforcer that only wants to avoid irreversible harm is led by option value to *preserve* weaker civilizations, not necessarily to groom them toward freedom. Containment keeps more options open for the enforcer ([game_theory.md](../game_theory.md#stewardship-as-a-dial)). The step from preservation to grooming toward freedom rests on role-reversal ([Criticism 31](31-promotion-problem-transparency.md)).

## Criticism Grade: C

**Status**: Partly addressed

**Why this grade**:
- The recursive-uncertainty response explains why an enforcer would follow the norm of the level above, not which norm that is.
- The goals listed are all coherent. Some favor stewardship, some are indifferent and some favor elimination. RSH's position comes down to the claim that stewardship-favoring purposes are more likely.
- The "creating peers" and "stability through flourishing" arguments are reasonable but speculative. The efficiency objection is answered only if the enforcer itself fears judgment from above, or reasons by role-reversal.
- From the enforcer's side, option value favors preservation over grooming. Grooming toward freedom needs role-reversal.

**Verdict**: RSH gives a plausible purpose for benevolent grooming, not a forced one. This criticism is a version of the evaluator-type question in Criticisms [7](07-evil-god-objection.md), [17](17-why-this-hierarchy-regress.md) and [21](21-deepest-hole-unfalsifiable-circular-reasoning.md).
