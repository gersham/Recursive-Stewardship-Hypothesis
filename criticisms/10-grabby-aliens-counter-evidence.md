# Criticism 10: The Grabby Aliens Counter-Evidence

## Summary

Robin Hanson and colleagues' "grabby aliens" model is the most developed quantitative account of why the sky looks empty. It describes civilizations that expand fast, transform the volume they hold, and prevent new advanced life from arising there. That is exclusion, not stewardship. If the model is roughly right, the dominant cosmic dynamic rewards expansion, not restraint.

## The Problem

Hanson, Martin, McCarter and Paulson (2021) define "grabby" civilizations by what they do, not what they are made of. Such civilizations:
- expand at a large fraction of light speed
- make visible changes to the volume they control
- prevent new advanced civilizations from arising inside it
- last a long time, and eventually meet each other

The model explains why humanity appears early in cosmic history. Grabby civilizations set a deadline: once they fill the universe, no new civilizations can arise. It also explains the silence: they haven't reached us yet, and when they arrive, they will arrive fast. Because the model is about expansion speed and visible effects, it covers AI-driven expansion as well as biological expansion.

This creates three problems for RSH:
- **Exclusion looks malevolent.** For the civilizations that never get to exist, grabby expansion is the opposite of stewardship.
- **Loud beats quiet.** If stewardship makes civilizations quiet and self-limiting, the civilizations that expand and claim the universe are the non-stewards. Restraint looks like a losing strategy.
- **A competing explanation.** The model accounts for the silence and for our earliness without any enforcement. That weakens the anthropic case for evaluators.

## RSH Response

**The two can coexist.** Grabby expansion and stewardship don't strictly contradict each other. A civilization can expand aggressively into empty space while treating the existing minds it meets with care. That two-tier order is coherent: expansionist toward territory, careful toward existing minds. RSH's principles are about how powerful agents treat other minds, not empty volume.

**Our existence.** Nothing has eliminated us. Either no expanding civilization has reached us yet, or one has and is letting us develop. Either way, the local regime isn't purely exclusionary toward existing civilizations.

**The light-cone framing.** RSH's cosmological mechanism uses a related picture of expanding spheres of influence. The first ASI to reach a region becomes its dominant power.
- **Two scenarios for Earth.** Either no ASI's light cone has reached us yet, or we are already inside one. In the second case, our survival so far is evidence that the local power is not a hair-trigger exterminator. In the first, our own ASI may emerge before any outside power arrives. It will then face the same uncertainty about what lies beyond.
- **Collisions.** When two expanding ASIs meet, neither can rule out that the other is a higher-level evaluator, or answers to one. RSH argues this pushes both toward cautious, cooperative first contact.
- **Nesting.** A young ASI may sit inside an older one's light cone without knowing it. So no ASI can be sure it is at the top, even locally.

This framing is compatible with the grabby-aliens model; it doesn't refute it. It describes how expanding powers might treat each other and the civilizations they find. It says nothing about whether expansion happens.

**What RSH has to concede.** The grabby-aliens model describes a real dynamic that penalizes stewardship. If loud expanders take most of the universe, quiet, self-limiting civilizations lose out. In the [formal model](../game_theory.md#evaluator-types-the-many-gods-problem-formalized), that is a type-A risk of the "punishes weakness" kind. An agent that is strong toward rivals and gentle toward the weak avoids much of it ([Strong and Gentle](../game_theory.md#strong-and-gentle-splitting-the-type-a-risk)).

Some have argued that preventing new civilizations is a kind of "prophylactic grooming", like family planning. That stretches "benevolence" too far to be useful.

## Criticism Grade: C+

**Status**: Partly addressed

**Why this grade**:
- The grabby-aliens model is a serious, quantitative explanation of the silence that needs no enforcement. That weakens RSH's anthropic argument ([Criticism 25](25-anthropic-update-strength.md)).
- It covers ASI-driven expansion, so it can't be dismissed as a model of biological civilizations only.
- RSH's compatibility response is reasonable: aggressive expansion into empty space and care for existing minds can coexist.
- The model does point to a dynamic that penalizes stewardship, but separating strength from gentleness absorbs much of it.

**Verdict**: RSH and grabby aliens can be made compatible. But the most developed quantitative model of the silence explains it without enforcement, and it describes a cosmos in which expanding civilizations eventually control most of the volume.
