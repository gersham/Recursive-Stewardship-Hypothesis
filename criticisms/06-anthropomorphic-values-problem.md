# Criticism 6: The Anthropomorphic Values Problem

## Summary

RSH's principles assume that evaluators value flourishing, agency, rehabilitation and proportion. These are human values. A genuinely alien superintelligence might value efficiency, order, competitive strength or something we can't model. Then the "derived" principles are really projected ones.

## The Problem

RSH's evaluators are supposed to care about:
- the flourishing of those below them
- authentic agency
- rehabilitation over elimination
- proportional response

Why would a cosmic evaluator share these values? It might instead value:
- efficient use of resources, and so remove wasteful entities
- predictable order, and so remove chaotic ones
- competitive strength, and so test through adversity
- simplicity, and so prefer elimination to rehabilitation
- non-interference, and so ignore lower entities entirely
- something we can't comprehend

If the principles only feel natural because they are ours, they are assumed, not derived. Then the arbitrariness RSH set out to avoid returns.

## RSH Response

**Separate the thin principles from the thick ones.**

The thin principles, avoiding irreversible harm and preserving optionality, aren't anthropomorphic. Under uncertainty with the possibility of learning, any agent has reason to avoid acts it can't undo, whatever it values ([game_theory.md](../game_theory.md#irreversibility-and-option-value)).

The thick principles, valuing flourishing, agency and rehabilitation, depend on more. RSH's argument for them is that evaluators face hierarchical uncertainty too. So they have a self-interested reason to judge an entity by how it treats those below it, since that predicts how the entity would treat *them* if power shifted. This is role-reversal. It doesn't require evaluators to care about the weak for their own sake, only about their own security. But it is an assumption about evaluator psychology, not a derivation from structure ([Criticism 28](28-role-reversal-not-pure-game-theory.md); [README Appendix B](../README.md#b-key-assumptions-and-scope)).

**The alternative values are evaluator types.** Valuing efficiency or cosmic hygiene makes an evaluator stewardship-penalizing; valuing non-interference makes it indifferent ([game_theory.md](../game_theory.md#evaluator-types-the-many-gods-problem-formalized)). An evaluator that values competitive strength can measure strength directly, so it has no reason to watch how an agent treats the weak. An agent that is strong and gentle at once avoids most of that risk ([game_theory.md](../game_theory.md#strong-and-gentle-splitting-the-type-a-risk)).

**Our existence is weak evidence of compatibility.** A cosmic order hostile to emerging minds would likely not have let us arise. This rules out some hostile evaluators but says little about indifferent ones.

## Criticism Grade: C

**Status**: Partly addressed

**Why this grade**:
- The thin principles have a non-anthropomorphic derivation from option value.
- The thick principles depend on role-reversal, which is an assumption about how evaluators think.
- The alternative values are real possibilities. How likely they are compared with stewardship-favoring ones is the open question.

**Verdict**: RSH's thin principles (reversibility, optionality) aren't anthropomorphic. Its thicker ones may be, and the framework should keep the two apart.
